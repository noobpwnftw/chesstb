I

The clock is part of the position.

For more than three decades, the position above has been endgame theory's most famous record. White mates in 262 moves, but the rules end the game hundreds of plies before the mate. ChessTB stores the answer at every halfmove clock from 0 to 99.

THE LONGEST MATE FROM A FRESH CLOCK, SAME ENDGAME

Without the rule this is mate in 69. Within the rule it is mate in 84.

The fastest mate takes too long between captures, so it can't be played. To keep the clock alive, White has to take a longer route, and with a fresh clock, the longest forced mate in this endgame is 84 moves. Only one first move keeps it: Kb2.

SAME POSITION, DIFFERENT CLOCK

Move the clock. Watch the mate get longer, then disappear.

Black is lost. Without the rule, White mates in 94. Within it, White mates in 97, and that holds up to clock 74. After that the clock forces White to reach a capture or pawn move sooner, and those routes take longer to mate: 98 moves at clock 75, 105 at clock 77. From clock 78, Black holds the draw.

II

Castling, finally.

Endgame tables have always assumed nobody can castle. These positions show what that assumption hides. Each rook and king arrangement is indexed, so Chess960 is covered by the same tables.

KRKB · White to move8/8/3k4/8/8/8/8/R3K1b1 w Q - 0 1Open in explorer
ROOK AGAINST BISHOP

O-O-O+. A fork made by castling.

The rook lands on d1. It checks the king on d6 and, along the first rank the king has just left, attacks the bishop on g1. It's the only winning move, and it leads to mate in 17. Without the castling right, the same position is a draw.

KRKPP · White to move8/8/8/8/pk6/8/7p/R3K3 w Q - 0 1Open in explorer
ROOK AGAINST TWO PAWNS

The king is in the way. Castling moves it.

Black's h-pawn queens next move. The rook on a1 can't reach h1 because its own king is on e1. Castling moves the king and brings the rook to d1, covering h1, in one move. Out of 12 legal moves, it's the only one that wins. Kd2 clears the rank too, but one move slower, and only draws. Without the castling right, White can't win at all.

III

Every pawn push counted.

Captures and promotions change the material. Pawn pushes don't, but they can't be taken back. ChessTB counts how many pushes a win still needs before the next conversion, and finds the line that needs the fewest.
